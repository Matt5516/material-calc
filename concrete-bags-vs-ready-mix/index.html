Bagged concrete vs ready-mix: which is cheaper?

The cost crossover, with short-load fees included.

At typical 2026 prices (80 lb bags at $6.50, ready-mix at $170/yd³ plus a $150 short-load fee under 4 yd³), bags are cheaper below about 1.23 cubic yards, roughly a 10 × 10 ft slab at 4 in. Above that, a truck costs less and saves mixing 45 bags per yard by hand.

What else to weigh

  • Labor: one cubic yard is about 45 bags and 3,600 lb to lift, mix and place.
  • Cold joints: mixing bags slowly on a big slab can leave weak seams. A truck pours it all at once.
  • Truck access: chutes reach about 15–20 ft. Beyond that you need wheelbarrows or a pump, which costs extra.
  • Mixer rental: a drum mixer rents for about $50–80 a day and speeds up bag pours.

Typical 2026 US prices used in the tables: 80 lb concrete $6.50, ready-mix $170/yd³ plus a $150 short-load fee under 4 yd³; gravel $5.50 per 0.5 ft³ bag or $45/ton + $100 delivery; mulch $4 per 2 ft³ bag or $40/yd³ + $75 delivery; topsoil $3.25 per 0.75 ft³ bag or $35/yd³ + $75 delivery. FAQ

What is a short-load fee?

Ready-mix plants add a fee to small orders, often below 3–5 cubic yards, because a truck carries up to about 10. Ask your plant for its minimum.
